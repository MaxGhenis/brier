## American Family Act H.R. 2763 two-layer pilot — 2026-08-01

- Replacing §24 wholesale creates an implementation-specific dependency that a vehicle-independent atom does not naturally express. The `section-24-inapplicable-after-2024` atom is necessary to stop the replacement §§24A and 24B from stacking with legacy §24, but an otherwise equivalent vehicle that amended §24 in place would fail that atom.
- The amount and threshold atoms mix statutory bases with formulas. The $300 monthly amount is only the 2025 base; the under-6 and newborn amounts are multipliers of its indexed value. Likewise, $150,000 and $112,500 are initial-threshold bases that are already indexed for TY2025. The current atom schema has no native dependency or formula edge, so `parameters` and `interactions` must carry those qualifications.
- The initial phaseout is entangled with a structural rewrite. Its one-twelfth-of-5-percent formula has two caps, including a hypothetical allowance calculated by setting the “dollar amounts” in both §24A(b)(1)(A) and (B) to $166.67, although (B) is written as a percentage with a newborn override. Even the unambiguous annual multiplication of $166.67 is $2,000.04, not exactly $2,000. The decomposition preserves the literal substitution and its ambiguity rather than normalizing it.
- The §24A(b)(3)(B) initial-threshold indexing rule imports a clause written around “the calendar year in which such month begins” into a taxable-year rule. The intended CPI-from-2022 adjustment and $5,000 rounding are clear, but the face of the text does not supply a mechanically unique lookup convention for every non-calendar taxable year.
- PolicyEngine mapping is intentionally sparse. The allowed ARPA amount path is the closest structural match because it has under-6 and age-6-through-17 bands, but its $3,600/$3,000 schedule differs from the bill's pre-indexing $4,320/$3,600 annualized schedule. Separate allowed files best match the two phaseout rates and thresholds, the initial-phaseout cap, refundability, the lack of an earned-income threshold or refundable cap, TIN-versus-SSN rules, and the other-dependent amount and phaseout. These are conceptual matches, not full encodings: the bill's 5-percent monthly phaseouts are continuous while nearby PolicyEngine amount/increment parameters represent stepped reductions, and no allowed path captures the CPI formulas, newborn multiplier, claimant rules, or territorial branches.
- “Full refundability” is conditional in the text. It applies to allowances attributable to months in which the taxpayer, or either spouse filing jointly, has a principal abode in the United States or Puerto Rico for more than half the month. A single Boolean named `fully_refundable` can overstate the state if that residence condition is dropped.
- Presumptive eligibility defeats a clean operational/substantive split. The portal, enrollment, challenge, grace, and hardship machinery belongs at the operational provision layer, but §24A(c)(7) makes a §7527A presumptive period and some retroactive payment periods determine final specified-child status. The revision atom therefore depends on machinery deliberately omitted from the revision list.
- Territorial coverage is branching rather than scalar. Annual mirror-code reimbursement plus duplicate-credit denial and American Samoa's approved-benefit-plan route plus direct-credit fallback remain conditional territorial atoms. The separate §7527A advance-payment elections and $300,000 administration supplements were deliberately kept only in operational provision metrics, not revisions.
- Three textual mismatches should not be silently cured: §24A(e)(1) says “qualifying child” where the new credit otherwise uses “specified child”; §7527A(h)(6)(A)(ii) points to subsection (b)(4) although the Puerto Rico rule appears in (b)(5); and §7527A(h)(5)(E)(iii)(II) points to a mirror-code definition in §24(h) although the new definition appears in §24A(h)(1)(C). Section 24B's incorporated TIN rule inherits the first ambiguity.
- Effective dates are heterogeneous. General annual entitlement applies to taxable years beginning after 2024, ordinary monthly advances begin only for months after enactment, grace and hardship rules reach months after 2024, and disclosure authority begins on enactment. A flat AND bundle records the introduced-text concepts but cannot by itself represent that timing graph.
- The only local cross-bill reuse encoded is the coarse TY2027 predicate “refundable CTC earned-income threshold ≤ $1” shared with S.3596. Under H.R. 2763 the bill text contains no earned-income floor from TY2025; S.3596 sets a $1 threshold from TY2026 while retaining its 15-percent phase-in and refundable cap. The shared atom is not a claim that the bills share full refundability or effective dates.
- Observed SOI hints are fragile under a wholesale section replacement. `irs.soi.additional_child_tax_credit_returns` and `irs.soi.ctc.qualifying_children` are observed Thesis concepts, but IRS could rename, split, or discontinue those lines when §24A replaces §24. The provision metrics label them as transition benchmarks and leave finer operational series null.
- The under-6 schedule resists a single clean age-band atom because the 120-percent state is false for a child under one month old. The decomposition therefore qualifies the under-6 atom and keeps the 800-percent newborn override separate; their interaction carries the shared indexed base.
- The supplied raw bill cache is one 65,221-character physical line with no newline terminators. Quote validation can still use its normalized alphanumeric stream, but heading-at-line-start section extraction may not recover the site's full-section disclosure. The raw input was left unchanged.
- Section 24A(c)(1)'s specified-child criteria contain no familial-relationship requirement. That is an unusually broad literal state, not an inferred drafting correction; the revision says so expressly while the provision analysis leaves implementation consequences open.
- Section 24A(f)(1) mixes taxable-year credit eligibility with disallowance periods measured from the most recent calendar month “for which” a final determination exists. The atom preserves the literal 120- and 24-month definitions but does not resolve which taxable years that period partially overlaps.
- Section 24B(c)(2) operates by applying §152(b)(3)(A) without the text after “resident of the United States.” The atom preserves that textual operation instead of silently deciding every contiguous-country-residence edge case.
- Section 24A(c)(6) says “rules similar to the rules section 152(e),” omitting “of,” and does not say how §152(e)'s annual divorced-parent rule maps to monthly specified-child status. The atom preserves the open-ended “rules similar” formulation rather than supplying that mapping.
- The phaseout decomposition needed separate rate, threshold, and cap atoms to use the singular `peParameterPath` field honestly. Even after that split, a single PolicyEngine path is only a best match: the model paths do not encode the full monthly formula, and the $50-per-$1,000 other-dependent reduction still combines an amount with an increment.
- `revisions`, `interactions`, and `bundleCondition` are additive pilot fields with no frozen TypeScript type, renderer, or repository validator. This artifact uses `revisionIds` plus `series_hint` and `mechanism` for interactions, and a versioned `AND` object for the bundle; the schema cannot currently represent effective-date graphs or logical dependencies beyond that flat conjunction. Ad hoc checks are therefore the only present guard for exact revision keys, parameter-path allowlisting, provision and interaction references, and complete bundle membership.
